Understand the risk

If your most important person was no longer there tomorrow, what would happen?

An unexpected death can affect the people and financial commitments that keep a business running.

  • Loss of important revenue or customer relationships
  • Difficulty replacing specialist knowledge or leadership
  • Ongoing salaries, loans and financial commitments
  • Recruitment and training costs
  • Pressure on owners, partners and families

Key lesson: Business growth is important. Business continuity planning is equally important.

The core concept

What Is Key Person Insurance?

Key Person Insurance (also called Keyman Insurance) is a life insurance arrangement intended to help protect a business against financial consequences arising from the death of someone essential to its success.

Under a qualifying arrangement, a business generally takes out and owns a policy on an eligible key person's life, pays the premiums and receives the death benefit, subject to the policy terms. The funds may help manage disruption, meet commitments or recruit a replacement.

Important: This does not replace every business loss. Product availability, insurable interest, eligibility, ownership, beneficiary rights and coverage must be verified with the insurer.

Who may be essential?

Who Is a Key Person in Your Business?

A key person is someone whose loss could create significant financial or operational consequences. The role alone does not establish eligibility.

Founder or owner

Key relationships, strategic decisions and leadership.

Director or executive

Core management and business continuity.

Specialist professional

Skills or qualifications that may be difficult to replace.

Critical employee

Essential customer, technical or revenue responsibilities.

Beyond one policy

Plan for Business Continuity

Key person protection is one part of broader risk management. Business owners should also review succession arrangements, emergency liquidity, loans and obligations, ownership agreements and the financial needs of their families.

Partnership and ownership succession arrangements may require separate legal documentation and appropriately structured life cover. These are not automatically included in a Key Person Insurance policy.

Tax awareness in India

Key Person Insurance and Taxation

Tax treatment matters, but it should not be the sole reason for considering business protection.

Premiums

Premiums for a genuine qualifying business-purpose Keyman policy may be deductible as business expenditure, subject to applicable law and the facts of the case. Deductibility is not automatic.

Policy proceeds

Keyman policy proceeds are generally excluded from the usual life-insurance exemption under section 10(10D) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Section 28(vi) addresses their taxation as business income under that Act.

Ownership and assignment

Assignments, business structure, recipient and policy terms can change the analysis. Review the relevant tax year and applicable legislation.

Professional review

Discuss potential tax consequences with a qualified chartered accountant before deciding. No deduction or tax outcome is guaranteed.

Tax information is general educational content, not individual tax advice. Section references shown above relate to the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Income-tax Act, 2025 has a different section structure for applicable tax years. Confirm the relevant law, transition rules, and policy treatment with a qualified tax professional before acting.

Official references: Income Tax Department — Section 10(10D) · Income Tax Department — Section 28(vi).
